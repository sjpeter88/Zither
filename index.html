Getting started

Press Tap to begin. Sound can only start after a touch, click or key press, so the instrument stays silent until then. After that, touch or click any string to pluck it, or press the gold play button to hear a piece from the song library.

Everything you change — settings, chord type, view, tempo and the last piece you chose — is remembered the next time you open the instrument on the same device.

The instrument

This is a concert zither in Munich tuning with 42 strings. Each string is modelled separately, and strings ring in sympathy with one another as they do on a real zither.

The accompaniment and bass strings are arranged in fifths, not in scale order. The red strings are F, A and C♯ and serve as landmarks. Note names are printed under the open strings near the right-hand bridge (you can hide them in Settings).

Playing by touch

The instrument is made for several fingers at once.

  • Fret and pluck: hold a finger on the fretboard to stop a melody string, and pluck the same string with another finger in the gap between the last fret and the bridge. With nothing held, the string sounds open.
  • Slide: keep the fretting finger down and drag it along the string. The pitch follows fret by fret without a new pluck.
  • Bend: drag the fretting finger across the string, away from its line. The note rises by up to two semitones.
  • Vibrato: rock the fretting fingertip gently back and forth along the string without leaving the fret. The pitch wavers with your finger.
  • Tremolo: pluck a string and keep your finger resting on it. After a moment the string is plucked rapidly over and over until you lift the finger. This works on the melody strings (in the plucking gap) and on the open strings.
  • Hammer-on and pull-off: while a string is ringing, put down a second finger on a higher fret, or lift it again.
  • Stop a note: lift the fretting finger.
  • Open strings: tap a string to pluck it. Sweep a finger across the strings for a glissando; a faster sweep plays louder.
  • Strum the melody strings: sweep across them in the plucking gap.

If Touching a fret sounds the note is on in Settings (the default), a touch on the fretboard plays the note straight away, which is easiest when starting out. Turn it off to play the traditional way, where the fretting hand is silent and only the plucking hand sounds the string.

Playing with a mouse

  • Click a fret to play that note; it rings on after you release.
  • Click an open string to pluck it. Hold the button and drag across strings to strum.
  • Hold a fret and drag along the string to slide, or across it to bend.
  • Hold the button down on an open string, or on a melody string in the plucking gap, for a tremolo.
  • Drag the background (or use the right button anywhere) to turn the view. Use the wheel to zoom.

Chords

The bar along the bottom plays chords on the open strings.

  • Major · Minor · 7th sets the chord type.
  • The twelve chord buttons are in the same order as the accompaniment strings. Pressing one lights the strings of that chord: the accompaniment strings and the bass string of its root.
  • Chord · Oom-pah · Waltz · Arp chooses how the chord is plucked: all together; bass then chord; bass, chord, chord; or bass followed by the chord strings one by one.
  • Auto-pluck on: choosing a chord plays it. Off: the strings are only lit, so you can pluck them yourself.
  • Keep it going: hold a chord button down and the pattern repeats in tempo, alternating the bass between the root and the fifth, until you let go; it finishes the bar it is in. Press another chord to change.
  • Latch on: the pattern keeps playing after you let go, leaving both hands free for the melody. Press the same chord again, or Damp, to stop it.
  • Metronome clicks at the chord pattern tempo, with an accent on the first beat of each bar (2, 3 or 4 beats a bar, set in Settings). While it runs, chord patterns start on its next beat so they stay in time. It switches off when you start a piece. Key: B.
  • Damp silences all strings, stops a running pattern and clears the lit chord.

The speed of the patterns is set by Chord pattern tempo in Settings.

Because the strings are in fifths, a major chord always has the same shape: the root string, the string next to it, and the string four further on.

Song library

Press the song button at the top left to open the library, and choose a piece to play it. While it plays, markers show the fretted notes, the plucked strings flash, and the current chord is lit on the strings and in the chord bar. You can play along at any time.

  • Transport: previous piece, play/pause, stop, next piece, repeat and record.
  • Repeat has three settings, shown by a small label on the button: off; 1, which repeats the current piece; and all, which plays every piece in the library one after another and starts again after the last. Play all, on repeat at the top of the library does the same from the first piece. Press Stop to end it.
  • Position: click or tap the progress line to jump to another place.
  • Tempo: the slider beside the metronome icon slows a piece to 60% or speeds it up to 130%.
  • Loop a passage (A–B): press A–B during or before the passage; the loop starts at the beginning of that bar. Press it again at the end of the passage; the loop ends at the end of that bar, and the passage is shaded on the progress line. The piece then repeats that passage until you press A–B a third time. It works with every practice mode, including Wait for me.
  • Speed trainer: turn on Speed trainer in Settings, set the tempo slider low (say 70%) and loop a passage with A–B. Each time the loop repeats it is played 5% faster, until it reaches full speed.
  • Count-in: with Count in one bar before a piece starts on in Settings, the play button gives one bar of clicks first, accented on the first beat, at the piece's tempo.

All pieces are public-domain compositions in arrangements made for this instrument. Each carries a label saying how its notes were obtained: From score (transcribed from a public-domain edition), Read from scan (read by eye from a scanned score, so small misreadings are possible) or From memory (approximate, not checked against a score). The selected piece also shows its source.

Changing the key. At the top of the library, Key of this piece shows the key of the piece you have chosen. Press − or + to move it down or up a semitone at a time (as far as six either way), for instance to suit a singer or another instrument. Melody, chords, tablature and notation all follow, and the setting is remembered for that piece. Original puts it back.

Grace notes. Some pieces have short ornamental notes before a main note. The zither plays them the way a player would: a quick pluck of the grace note, then the finger hammers on or pulls off to the main note on the same string. In ABC notation they are written in curly brackets, for example {A}B.

Finding a piece. Type in the search box at the top of the library to find pieces by title, composer, kind (for example "polka" or "German") or key. Tap the star beside a piece to make it a favourite, and ★ Favourites to show only your favourites.

Practice record. Each time you finish a piece with Wait for me on, the library notes it under the piece: how many times you have practised it, and your best result — the share of notes you got right first time and the shortest average wait — from run-throughs where you played at least nine notes in ten. The record is kept on this device.

Adding your own songs. Add a song… at the top of the library opens a box where you can paste a tune in ABC notation, a plain-text format widely used for folk music. The melody is arranged for the fretboard, and if the tune includes chord symbols the bass and accompaniment are added. Your songs appear under My Songs, are kept on this device only, and can be removed from the list when selected.

Recording. Press the round record button, play, and press it again to finish. A small bar appears with Play take, which replays your playing on the instrument, and Save audio, which saves it as a WAV sound file. If a song is playing while you record, it is recorded too. The latest take is kept on this device, even after you close the app, until you record another or discard it with ×. To keep a take for good, press Keep and give it a name: it then appears in the library under My Recordings, where you can play it, save it as audio, or remove it. (Play all skips recordings.)

Moving your songs to another device. Songs you add and recordings you keep live on one device. Export mine in the library saves them all to one file (on the iPad it opens the share sheet, so you can save it to Files or AirDrop it). On the other device, Import file… loads that file; anything already there is skipped. Import file… also accepts a plain .abc tune file.

Practising

The note button in the top bar opens the practice strip under it.

Tab or notes. The button beside Wait for me switches the strip between tablature and standard notation. Your choice is remembered.

Tablature. The strip shows the melody of the current piece scrolling from right to left past a fixed line. The five lines are the five melody strings in the same order as on screen, with the string nearest you at the bottom. Each circle is a note and the number in it is the fret (0 is the open string); the bar behind it shows how long the note lasts. Chord names run along the top and thin vertical lines mark the bars. Where two notes sound together, the one with the bolder ring is the tune.

Standard notation. The melody on a treble staff, in the key of the piece (including any transposition), with note heads, stems, dots, ledger lines and accidentals; grace notes are shown small before their note. It is simplified: notes are not beamed together, and short notes have no flags, so read the rhythm from the spacing as well. Fret numbers are only in the tablature.

Who plays.

  • Listen — the app plays everything.
  • I play melody — the app plays the bass and chords and leaves the tune to you. It still shows each note on the fretboard, and still plays the lower harmony notes under the tune.
  • I play chords — the app plays the melody and leaves the open strings to you. The strings of each chord light up when it is due, and the chord bar shows its name.

Wait for me (with I play melody) makes the song stop at every melody note until you pluck it. The note is shown on the fretboard a moment early, the string glows while the song is waiting, and the strip says your turn. Any way of sounding the right pitch counts: the lit fret, the same note on another string, or a melody key. Wrong notes are simply ignored, so you can take as long as you need. Without Wait for me the song keeps its own tempo; use the tempo slider to slow it down.

With I play chords, Wait for me stops the song at the start of each bar, and at each chord change, until you pluck the bass string of the chord, which glows amber. The bass note in either of the two lower groups counts, and so does pressing the chord's button in the chord bar (in any pattern that starts with the bass). Between those points the song runs on, and the chord strings stay lit for you to pluck in rhythm.

Score. With Wait for me on, the right-hand end of the strip counts the notes you have played out of the notes in the piece, the wrong notes, and how long the song waited for you on average. A summary appears when the piece ends. Start the piece again to reset it.

Zoom while practising. If you have zoomed in, the view follows what you need to play next: the fretboard for melody notes, the chord strings for bass notes.

Views

  • Play — the normal playing angle, as if seated at the instrument.
  • Top — straight from above, which gives the widest spacing between strings.
  • Show — a slowly moving display view, best for watching a piece play.

The Zoom button steps through four framings: the whole instrument; the fretboard alone; the accompaniment and bass strings (the ones chords are played on), which gives the widest spacing; and all the open strings. The zoomed framings are shown from above and make the strings much easier to hit on a small screen. Strings that are out of view still sound, so the chord buttons and song playback keep working. Choosing Play, Top or Show returns to the whole instrument.

Settings

Open Settings with the slider icon at the top right.

  • Volume — overall loudness.
  • Room — how much room reverberation surrounds the instrument.
  • Sympathetic resonance — how strongly strings set one another ringing.
  • Chord pattern tempo — the speed of the chord patterns, in beats per minute.
  • Vibrato on long notes — adds a gentle vibrato to longer melody notes in song playback and on the melody keys.
  • Vibrato depth — how wide the vibrato is, both the automatic kind and the kind you make by rocking your finger.
  • Tremolo — turns the rest-on-the-string tremolo on or off, and sets how long you must rest before it starts. Lengthen the delay if tremolo starts when you do not want it.
  • Mirror instrument — flips the zither left to right for left-handed playing.
  • Note names under the strings — shows or hides the printed note names.
  • Touching a fret sounds the note — see Playing by touch.

Sound, tuning and looks

These are in Settings and are remembered on each device.

  • Sound — four voicings of strings and soundbox: Concert (spruce top, steel strings; the default), Mellow (a darker rosewood body and softer strings), Brilliant (new strings with a bright attack) and Resonant (a long, ringing sustain).
  • Fretboard tuning — Munich (a′ a′ d′ g c, the default) or Viennese (a′ d′ g′ g c). The open strings stay in Munich tuning either way, as many players do, so the chord layout is unchanged. Songs are re-arranged for the new fretboard automatically.
  • Concert pitch — the frequency of the A above middle C, from 430 to 446 Hz (440 is standard; many Alpine ensembles tune to 442 or 443). Every string follows, including strings that are ringing.
  • Finish — the wood of the soundbox: amber sunburst spruce, natural spruce with maple sides, walnut, or black lacquer with gold purfling. This changes only the look.
  • Show hands during playback — when a song or a recording plays, a thumb with its metal ring plucks the melody strings, three fingertips pluck the open strings, and fingertips press the frets. They fade away when the music stops and never appear while you play yourself.

MIDI keyboard

On a computer, in Chrome, Edge or the Windows app, you can play the zither from a MIDI keyboard. Connect it, open Settings and press Connect next to MIDI keyboard. The browser may ask for permission first. After that it reconnects by itself whenever you start the instrument.

  • Keys below C3 (the C an octave below middle C) choose a chord: the note's name is the chord's root, and the chord type and pattern come from the chord bar. Hold the key to keep the pattern going, as with the chord buttons.
  • C3 and above play the melody, placed on the fretboard. Notes the fretboard cannot reach are moved by octaves into its range. How hard you strike the key sets how hard the string is plucked.
  • The sustain pedal lets melody notes ring after you release the keys, the pitch-bend wheel bends the last melody note up by as much as two semitones, and the modulation wheel adds vibrato to the notes you are holding.
  • MIDI notes count in Wait for me, like notes played on the screen.

Safari on iPad and iPhone has no MIDI support, so this is not available there.

iPad and Windows

iPad: open the instrument's web address in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. Opened from the home screen it runs full screen, and after the first visit it also works without an internet connection. Hold the iPad in landscape.

Windows: run ConcertZither.exe. Nothing needs installing. On a touch-screen PC, touch works as on the iPad.

The version number is shown on the start screen, at the top of the song library and of this manual, and at the bottom of Settings. After an update, compare it on each device to check it has the new version.

If something is wrong

  • No sound: touch the instrument once more (the device may have paused audio while the app was in the background), then check Volume in Settings and the device's own volume.
  • Strings ring on too long or sound muddy: press Damp, or lower Sympathetic resonance.
  • Open strings are hard to hit: use Zoom (the Chords framing is the roomiest), or use the chord buttons with Auto-pluck off to light the strings you want.
  • Imported songs did not arrive: the file must be one made by Export mine, or an ABC tune. Items with the same name and origin as ones already in the library are skipped rather than duplicated.
  • Save audio does nothing: rendering needs a recent browser. If it fails a message says so; Play take still works.
  • The top bar takes two rows: on narrower screens it wraps rather than hiding buttons. Turn the device to landscape or widen the window for a single row.

Add a song

Paste one tune in ABC notation. The melody is placed on the fretboard, and if the tune has chord symbols in quotes (such as "G" or "D7") the bass and accompaniment are added from them. The song is kept on this device under My Songs.

Understood: note names and octaves, sharps, flats and naturals, note lengths, rests, ties, triplets, dotted pairs (> and <), chords in [brackets], repeats with first and second endings, key and metre changes. Grace notes in {curly brackets} are played as ornaments. Only the first voice of a multi-voice tune is used; lyrics and other ornaments are ignored. Please add only music you have the right to use.
